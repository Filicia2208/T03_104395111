TELEVISION ENERGY DATA

Television Energy Insights

The following questions explore television energy consumption in the Australian market. Charts will be added to each section once the data analysis is complete. The objective is to help Australian consumers make informed decisions about technology, size, brand, and efficiency.

01

What type of TV screen technologies are currently available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

Brief Explanation

Three screen technologies are currently available in Australia: LCD, LCD (LED), and OLED. LCD (LED) is by far the most frequent, accounting for 3,794 of available models (83.02%), followed by LCD (484 models, 10.59%) and OLED (292 models, 6.39%).

02

What screen sizes are available and which are most frequent?

Brief Explanation

Screen sizes range from around 16 inches up to 116 inches. The most frequent sizes cluster around 55 inches, 65 inches, and 75 inches with 65 inches being the single most common size, closely followed by 55 inches and 75 inches.

03

Which brands have the largest number of models?

Brief Explanation

KOGAN has the largest number of models (807), followed by LG (723), Samsung Electronics (699), Samsung (497), and Hisense (327). These top five brands make up for the bulk of models on the market, with a steep difference after Hisense.

04

Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

Brief Explanation

LCD consumes the least power, with a median average mode power of 71.35W. LCD (LED) uses more (106W median) and OLED uses the most (112.70W median).

05

What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

Brief Explanation

There is a positive relationship. It is evident that as screen size increases, average power consumption increases as well. Small TVs typically have lower power usage, while large TVs over 100 inches can use around 300 to 500W or more.

06

What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

Brief Explanation

Star ratings are spread fairly evenly across all screen sizes. This means that larger TVs are not necessarily less energy-efficient than smaller ones as efficient design matters more than size alone.

07

Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

Brief Explanation

Yes. Spark Electronics has the highest average power consumption (233.45W), well ahead of Emete (182.03W) and the rest of the top 10, which range from 155.04W down to 130.03W. The box plot also shows that some brands like Kogan and Samsung have a wide spread of power values, while others have a much narrower, more consistent range.
